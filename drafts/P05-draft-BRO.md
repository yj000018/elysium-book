# P05 — Agency

## 1. Action as a civilizational condition

A civilization does not only possess a material base and a living field. It acts. It converts time, skill, attention, energy and matter into goods, services, knowledge and new possibilities. Every road in Material Base and every harvest in Vitality is also the trace of organized human action: someone designed it, financed it, built it, maintained it and decided that it was worth the effort.

Agency is the ELYSIUM foundation that reads this capacity to act. In the reference map, it concerns **the mechanisms of value creation, work, innovation and economic exchange**, and its function is to mobilize human potential and coordinate productive action. The term is deliberate. It is not "Economy", although economy is one of its facets. It names the power to act before naming the structures that channel that power.

This distinction matters. An economy can grow while the agency of most participants contracts. A financial system can become more liquid while the capacity of a community to decide what it produces becomes weaker. A labour market can be efficient in its own terms while large parts of the work that sustains life remain unrecognized. The diagnostic question is therefore not simply whether output is rising. It is broader: **which arrangements allow human potential to become action that creates value, and for whom, at what cost, and toward which ends?**

Agency must not be reduced to markets, nor romanticized as pure individual initiative. It is a coupled field of capacities and channels. Capacities belong to persons and collectives; channels are the economic, financial and productive architectures through which those capacities are mobilized, rewarded, constrained or wasted.

## 2. Six facets, one field of action

Agency contains six working facets: Economy, Work, Finance, Entrepreneurship, Innovation and Production. Each is a distinct lens; none is self-contained.

Economy concerns the architecture of exchange and allocation: how a society decides what is produced, how it circulates and how value is recognized. Work concerns human effort itself, paid and unpaid, and its relation to dignity, identity, subsistence and meaning. Finance concerns the instruments through which future capacity is mobilized in the present: credit, investment, money, risk and return. Entrepreneurship concerns the initiation of new ventures and the capacity to assemble resources around an intention. Innovation concerns the generation and diffusion of new methods, tools and forms of organization. Production concerns the physical and organizational transformation of inputs into outputs, and therefore the point where Agency touches Material Base most directly.

These facets form a circuit rather than a hierarchy. Finance enables entrepreneurship; entrepreneurship organizes work; work drives production; production feeds the economy; the economy determines what finance will reward next. Innovation runs across the whole circuit, changing what each part can do. A disturbance in one facet propagates through the others.

The labels remain working labels of Taxonomy A. They are analytical handles, not a final economic classification, and not a claim that every school of economic thought would divide the field the same way.

## 3. Reading Agency through the conceptual layer

The conceptual layer A gives Agency a complete diagnostic sequence.

**Definition** asks what agency means in this architecture: the capacity of persons and collectives to create value through work, exchange, initiative and invention. **Function** asks what this capacity enables: subsistence, coordination, specialization, creativity and the renewal of every other foundation. **Current State** examines how value creation is presently organized, without treating any single national or sectoral configuration as the global condition. **Historical Evolution** traces how wage labour, corporate forms, monetary systems, industrial production and digital platforms became what they are, so that none of them appears natural or inevitable.

**Core Pathologies** identifies structural failures. The reference map names three: financialization, alienation from labour and extractive economics. These are diagnostic categories to be tested, not verdicts to be asserted. **Feedback Loops** asks how a pattern reinforces itself: how returns on capital can concentrate the means of further investment, how precarious work can reduce the time and security required for initiative, or how metrics that ignore depletion can reward activities that deplete. **Dependencies** makes visible what Agency requires: energy and materials, healthy bodies, trust, rules, knowledge and a sense of purpose.

**Cross-Foundation Couplings** prevents Agency from becoming an economics chapter detached from the rest of civilization. **Leverage Points** asks where a change in ownership, incentive, measurement, institution or practice could shift a pattern. **Regenerative Alternatives** describes healthier directions without presenting them as achieved. **Transition Pathways** sequences change through experiments, institutions and learning. **Metrics / Signals of Health** distinguishes established economic indicators, contextual measures and internal ELYSIUM criteria.

The editorial layer E organizes the facet matrices around current paradigms, crises, transitions in progress, the technological and AI catalyst, cultural and inner transition, projects, risks and resources. For Agency, the E-layer catalyst step is especially prominent, because automation and AI act directly on work and production. The E layer makes the chapter readable. It does not replace layer A, and the prominence of the AI catalyst in E must not be read as a claim that technology is the primary driver of Agency in A.

## 4. The six facets in relation

### Economy

Economy is the architecture through which a society coordinates exchange, allocates resources and recognizes value. Markets, households, states, commons and gift relations are all allocation mechanisms; the dominant arrangement in a given context is a historical outcome, not a law of nature.

An ELYSIUM reading asks what an economy counts and what it leaves uncounted. Aggregate measures of output record transactions; they do not by themselves record depletion, care, ecological repair or the distribution of benefit. This is a well-documented limitation of aggregate indicators, but the chapter must not convert it into a claim that any specific indicator is "false". The architectural point is narrower: **what a civilization measures shapes what its agency is mobilized toward.**

### Work

Work is human effort directed toward the creation of value. It includes employment, but also domestic labour, care, voluntary contribution, self-production and learning. The facet matrix describes work as the interface between the interiority of a person and external reality. That is a framework position within ELYSIUM; it is useful for analysis and is not an empirical finding.

The diagnostic task is to examine how a given arrangement connects effort, social usefulness and reward. Where that connection weakens, the matrices describe alienation, loss of meaning and exhaustion. Claims about the prevalence of burnout, the global health burden of long working hours or the share of "meaningless" jobs require a defined population, period, source and method. P05 records them as concerns for inquiry, not as closed findings.

### Finance

Finance is the capacity to bring future value into the present: to lend, invest, insure and pool risk. It is a powerful coordination technology. Without it, few large infrastructures, enterprises or innovations would exist.

The pathology named in the reference map, financialization, describes a condition in which financial returns become an end that disciplines the rest of the economy rather than a means that serves it. This is a contested analytical category with a substantial literature; P05 uses it as a diagnostic lens, not as a settled description of every financial system. Regenerative finance appears in the reference map as a direction. Its instruments, outcomes and failure modes remain to be documented case by case.

### Entrepreneurship

Entrepreneurship is the capacity to initiate: to perceive a possibility, assemble resources and take risk in its pursuit. It is one of the most visible expressions of individual and collective agency.

The facet must not be reduced to start-up culture or to private ventures. Cooperatives, social enterprises, public initiatives and community projects are also forms of entrepreneurship. The question is not who owns a venture but whether the architecture allows initiative to arise, to fail safely and to serve purposes beyond its own continuation. Purpose-driven enterprise is named as a regenerative direction; it remains a direction until purpose is shown to shape decisions under pressure.

### Innovation

Innovation is the generation and diffusion of new tools, methods and forms of organization. It changes what every other facet can do. It also changes the risks.

A civilization can innovate rapidly in one domain while stagnating in another. It can produce tools that extend agency and tools that concentrate it. The E-layer matrices give great attention to AI as a catalyst; P05 treats that attention as a signal of salience, not as a forecast. Claims about the scale of technological unemployment or productivity gains from AI remain speculative and must be qualified as such.

### Production

Production is the transformation of energy, materials, knowledge and labour into goods and services. It is where Agency becomes physically consequential, and where it couples most tightly with Material Base and Vitality.

A linear production pattern draws resources in, transforms them and discards what remains. A circular pattern seeks to return materials and to design for repair, reuse and regeneration. Distributed production is named in the reference map as a regenerative direction. Whether it reduces material throughput or simply relocates it depends on context and requires evidence.

## 5. Couplings across the seven foundations

Agency becomes civilizationally intelligible through its relations with the other foundations.

With **Material Base**, it connects production to energy, materials, infrastructure and mobility. Every act of production draws on the physical base; every infrastructure is the result of organized agency. The magnitude of a given dependency must be established in context.

With **Vitality**, it connects work to health, care, agriculture and the economic organization of life-sustaining activity. P04 already noted that the work of sustaining life is often economically organized without being fully recognized. P05 receives that observation as a shared analytical question, not as a proven universal claim.

With **Cohesion**, it examines how work, income and ownership shape belonging, trust and inequality. Economic arrangements can bind or fragment communities, but the mechanisms must be specified for each case.

With **Governance**, it examines property rights, labour law, monetary institutions, competition rules and the regulation of finance. Agency is always exercised within rules; the chapter does not prescribe one institutional form before examining context.

With **Vision**, it connects innovation to science, education, data and the capacity to anticipate. Knowledge expands what agency can do; it does not determine what agency should do.

With **Consciousness**, it asks what a civilization considers worth doing, how it understands success, effort and contribution, and whether value is reduced to price. This is an interpretive and ethical layer of ELYSIUM, not an empirical indicator.

## 6. A.Agency and B.Economy

The ELYSIUM Taxonomy Federation records a primary correspondence between `A.F03 Agency` and `B.F03 Economy` through `ETF-0007`, with high confidence. Both structures address value creation, work, innovation and economic exchange. It is not a synonym and not a fusion.

The non-equivalence note is decisive for this chapter: **A foregrounds human potential and agency; B foregrounds economic structure and inclusion.** Taxonomy A begins from the capacity to act and reads economic structure as one channel of that capacity. Taxonomy B begins from the economic system and asks how it can be sustainable and inclusive. Each sees something the other places in the background.

The secondary relation `ETF-S-003` connects `A.F03` to `B.F01 Knowledge`. It remains deferred. Its probable ground is Innovation, which in A sits inside Agency and in B touches a dedicated Knowledge foundation. P05 does not close this relation. The correct operation is comparison with scope notes, not silent normalization.

## 7. From extraction to regenerative agency

A transition in Agency begins by making the circuit of value legible. It asks where effort is spent, what it produces, who decides, who benefits, which costs are displaced onto bodies, communities or ecosystems, and which forms of initiative are blocked.

The reference map names three families of direction: purpose-driven enterprise, regenerative finance and distributed production. The facet matrices add others: plural activity combining paid and unpaid contribution, shared governance in organizations, circular production and human-centred uses of AI. These are families of direction, not a universal programme. Their value must be tested through evidence, context, feedback and reversibility.

The conceptual sequence keeps the transition disciplined. Historical Evolution prevents present economic forms from appearing natural. Core Pathologies prevents isolated symptoms, such as a single financial crisis, from being mistaken for structural causes. Dependencies prevents gains in one economy from being achieved by exporting depletion to another. Leverage Points focuses action on measurement, ownership, incentive and rule rather than on exhortation. Metrics prevents aspiration from presenting itself as proof.

The editorial layer adds projects, experiments, risks and resources. A successful cooperative or a regenerative investment fund is evidence of a local or sectoral achievement; it is not evidence that the economy as a whole has changed its architecture.

## 8. Passage toward the faceted matrices

P05 establishes the foundation-level map. The six F03 facet matrices exist in the ontology repository with status `RECOVERED_DRAFT_QA_PENDING`. They contain rich material but also strong, insufficiently sourced claims. The next step is not to import them, but to select faceted work according to a defined question, scale, evidence state and intended use.

A reader should be able to ask of any Agency passage:

1. Which facet is in focus?
2. At which scale is it being read?
3. Which layer—conceptual A or editorial E—is organizing the analysis?
4. Which statements are source-backed, derived, internal, provisional or unresolved?
5. Which relation to another foundation or taxonomy is being asserted?

If these questions remain answerable, Agency can function as a foundation rather than an economic manifesto. It becomes a way to read how a civilization turns human potential into action, while preserving uncertainty, provenance and the sovereignty of both taxonomies.

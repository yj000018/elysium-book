# P02 — The Civilizational Map

## 1. Why civilization needs a map

A civilization cannot be understood by listing its sectors as if they were independent machines. Energy, health, institutions, culture, knowledge, production, meaning and infrastructure continuously affect one another. A disturbance in one domain can propagate through several others; a regenerative intervention can fail when it ignores the system around it.

ELYSIUM therefore begins with a map rather than a catalogue. The map is a disciplined way of asking where a phenomenon belongs, what scale it affects, which foundations it touches, and how its present condition can change. It is not a claim that reality is reducible to a diagram. It is an instrument for making complex reality discussable without flattening it.

The architecture used by the Book is expressed as:

```text
3 scales × 7 foundations × 38 facets × 12 analytical steps
```

This formula defines a working grid. It does not exhaust the world, and it does not turn every domain into a closed category. It provides a common orientation for diagnosis, comparison and transition design.

## 2. Three scales of analysis

The primary Taxonomy A, the metabolic architecture used by the Book, reads civilization through three provisional scales.

### Individual / Micro

The Micro scale concerns personal agency, consciousness, embodied life and local metabolic interaction. It includes the conditions through which a person can act, learn, relate, stay healthy and participate in a living environment.

### Collective / Meso

The Meso scale concerns communities, institutions, organizations and regional cohesion. It is the scale at which relationships become systems, norms become practices, and shared resources are governed.

### Civilizational / Macro

The Macro scale concerns planetary infrastructure, biosphere interaction and civilizational direction. It includes the long-range patterns through which societies organize matter, energy, knowledge, power and meaning.

These labels are confirmed as provisional reference labels for Taxonomy A. They must not be silently imposed on Taxonomy B. In the federative relation, Taxonomy B uses its own vocabulary and includes a Universal dimension whose scope must not be reduced to A's Civilizational / Macro scale.

The value of the three scales is not separation. It is the ability to see how a local condition, a collective arrangement and a planetary trajectory interact without collapsing them into one level of explanation.

## 3. Seven foundations of civilizational structure

The seven foundations form the principal grammar of Taxonomy A.

1. **Material Base** concerns physical infrastructure, resources and spatial organization.
2. **Vitality** concerns biological and ecological health, regeneration and the conditions of life.
3. **Agency** concerns value creation, work, innovation and economic exchange.
4. **Cohesion** concerns relationships, culture, belonging and the social fabric.
5. **Governance** concerns law, justice, institutions, decision-making and collective order.
6. **Vision** concerns science, education, foresight and technological direction.
7. **Consciousness** concerns ethics, spirituality, worldview and meaning-making.

A foundation is not a department. It is a field of relations with a characteristic function, pathology and regenerative direction. For example, Mobility is located within the Material Base, but its consequences extend into Agency, Cohesion, Governance and Vitality. The foundation is therefore an anchor, not a boundary that prevents cross-reading.

The seven foundations belong to Taxonomy A's architecture. Taxonomy B remains a valid and autonomous structure with its own foundations, labels, scope and granularity.

## 4. Thirty-eight facets as units of work

The 38 facets provide operational detail inside the seven foundations. They allow the Book to move from civilizational abstractions to domains that can be observed, compared and acted upon.

The canonical reference map assigns each facet a stable ID, such as `F01_05_MOBILITE`, `F02_03_BIODIVERSITE` and `F02_04_ECOSYSTEMES`. The ID is a traceability device before it is a literary label: it lets a claim, matrix, source fragment, review decision and editorial passage point to the same working object.

The 38-facet map is the reference inventory for Taxonomy A. The current federation does not establish an enumerated list of 38 equivalent facets for Taxonomy B. Until such a list is evidenced, B remains explicitly `B_FACETS_UNENUMERATED` in relation to this map.

This boundary protects two forms of fidelity at once. It prevents the Book from losing the practical clarity of its 38-facet architecture, and it prevents the infrastructure taxonomy from being treated as an incomplete copy of the metabolic taxonomy.

## 5. Two layers of twelve-step analysis

The Book now distinguishes two related but non-identical twelve-step layers. This distinction is necessary because the conceptual matrix in `ontology-map.md` and the editorial protocol used by the pilot matrices serve different functions.

### Conceptual layer A — ontology map

The conceptual layer defines the analytical architecture of a facet:

1. **Definition** — What is this domain?
2. **Function** — What is its role in civilizational metabolism?
3. **Current State** — Where does it stand today?
4. **Historical Evolution** — How did it become this way?
5. **Core Pathologies** — What are its structural failures?
6. **Feedback Loops** — How do these failures reinforce themselves or others?
7. **Dependencies** — What does this domain rely upon?
8. **Cross-Foundation Couplings** — How does it interact with the other foundations?
9. **Leverage Points** — Where are the critical nodes for intervention?
10. **Regenerative Alternatives** — What does a healthy architecture look like?
11. **Transition Pathways** — How do we move from the current to the regenerative state?
12. **Metrics / Signals of Health** — How do we measure success?

This layer is the conceptual reference for reasoning about structure, causality, transformation and measurement.

### Editorial layer E — derived protocol

The pilot matrices use a second sequence to organize readable editorial and research material:

1. **Definition / ontology** — What is this domain?
2. **Function / teleology** — What role does it play in civilizational metabolism?
3. **Current paradigm** — What arrangement currently dominates?
4. **Current crisis** — Where does the present arrangement fail or become unstable?
5. **Transitions in progress** — What is already changing?
6. **Technological and AI catalyst** — Which tools can alter coordination, knowledge or capacity?
7. **Cultural and inner transition** — Which habits, values or representations must change?
8. **Future paradigm** — What healthier architecture could emerge?
9. **Transition pathways** — Through which sequences can change become concrete?
10. **Projects and experiments** — Which situated practices make the transition testable?
11. **Risks and blind spots** — How could the transition reproduce harm or create new dependencies?
12. **Resources and library** — Which evidence, sources and experiences support the analysis?

This layer is a derived editorial protocol. It is not a one-to-one implementation of the conceptual layer A. For example, editorial “Current crisis” does not replace the historical evolution and core pathologies of A; it selects a readable entry point for a derived matrix.

The complete relation between the two layers is recorded in `12-STEP-LAYER-CROSSWALK-001.md`. The crosswalk is explicit, non-canonical and does not assert term equivalence.

A matrix is also not a source object. A source may provide evidence for one step, several steps or a contradiction. A derived matrix organizes and qualifies material for analysis; it must never be presented as recovered source text when its passages were reconstructed or reformulated.

## 6. Two taxonomies, one federative reading layer

ELYSIUM preserves two valid taxonomies.

**Taxonomy A — Metabolic** provides the Book's primary conceptual architecture: scales, foundations, facets and the conceptual layer A of the twelve-step analysis. The derived editorial layer E organizes the pilot matrices without becoming a second source taxonomy. A is oriented toward the metabolism and transition of civilization.

**Taxonomy B — Open Infrastructure** offers an autonomous structure with its own vocabulary, scope and granularity. It foregrounds dimensions that may not have a one-to-one place in Taxonomy A, including its own universal and infrastructural emphases.

The ELYSIUM Taxonomy Federation, or ETF, provides typed relations between the two. A relation may be a primary correspondence, a correspondence with a scope delta, a transversal relation without a strict equivalent, or a deferred secondary correspondence.

A correspondence is not a synonym. A shared label is not proof of an identical theory. A relation with high confidence can still carry a non-equivalence note. The correct reading is therefore federative: compare, translate cautiously, preserve the delta, and allow each taxonomy to retain its source authority.

This rule is especially important at the largest scale. A correspondence between A's Civilizational / Macro and B's Universal is explicitly a scope-delta relation. Universal must not be silently reduced to civilizational, and Consciousness in A must not be replaced by a partial match elsewhere.

## 8. Evidence, statuses and limits

P02 distinguishes at least five states:

- **Source** — a preserved source object or source fragment;
- **Derived** — a structured or reformulated child with explicit provenance;
- **Framework** — an ELYSIUM conceptual or analytical construction;
- **Review** — a claim whose wording, source sufficiency or scope remains open;
- **Founder decision** — an explicit authority decision that constrains later application.

The pilot does not silently close every claim. Four reservations remain visible: economic losses expressed in global GDP points, causal claims concerning zoonoses, the status of the Natural Capital Protocol, and the phrase “sixth mass extinction”. P02 must either retain these as reservations or use the approved cautious formulations; it must not strengthen them by prose momentum.

Likewise, the language of planetary boundaries must remain connected to its scientific source and must not become a set of simplistic binary thresholds. Axes of transition that are internal to ELYSIUM must be labelled as such when they are not empirical measurements.

The diagnostic brief and this controlled draft are therefore intentionally non-canonical. They are designed to be reviewed, corrected and versioned. Their authority comes from traceability and explicit decisions, not from fluency alone.

## 9. From P02 to the foundation chapters

P02 establishes the reading protocol for P03 through P09. Each foundation chapter can then develop its own structure while preserving the same map: scale, foundation, facets, cross-foundation relations, evidence status and transition logic.

The sequence is deliberately modular. P03 can develop Material Base without deciding the final meaning of Vitality in P04. P04 can work on Vitality without absorbing Environment from Taxonomy B. Later chapters can use ETF relations to compare perspectives without turning the Book into a flattened synthesis.

P02 is complete as a map when a reader can answer five questions:

1. What is being mapped?
2. At which scale is it being read?
3. Which foundation and facet are in focus?
4. Which analytical step is being performed?
5. Which statements are source-backed, derived, internal, provisional or unresolved?

The next phase is not to declare the map finished. It is to use the map carefully, expose its limits through the foundation chapters, and return improvements to the architecture through explicit versioning. ELYSIUM becomes durable not by pretending that every boundary is settled, but by making each boundary visible enough to be worked on.

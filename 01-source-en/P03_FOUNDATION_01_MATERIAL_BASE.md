# P03 — Material Base

## 1. The physical metabolism of civilization

A civilization does not exist above matter. It exists through flows of energy, water, materials, bodies, spaces and infrastructures. Every institution, market, school, hospital, household and network depends on a physical base, even when its visible activity appears symbolic or digital.

Material Base is therefore not a technical appendix to civilization. It is the field in which civilization becomes physically possible. It concerns the resources, structures and spatial arrangements through which collective life is sustained, organized and transformed.

This foundation must not be confused with a catalogue of objects. A road is not only a road. It is a decision about access, territory, energy, time, investment and social connection. A building is not only a building. It is a concentration of materials, labour, maintenance, memory and future obligation. A digital service is not immaterial: it depends on devices, electricity, data infrastructure, extraction and waste. The diagnostic question is always broader than the object itself: **what flows does this arrangement enable, constrain or externalize?**

In Taxonomy A, Material Base is the first foundation because it provides the physical conditions through which the other foundations can operate. It is not more important than Vitality, Agency, Cohesion, Governance, Vision or Consciousness. It is the material anchoring of their interaction.

## 2. Six facets, one field

Material Base contains six working facets: Energy, Water, Habitat, Infrastructure, Mobility and Materials. They are distinct lenses, not sealed departments. Each reveals a different part of the physical metabolism, while each also depends on the others.

Energy describes the capacity to move, heat, transform, produce and coordinate. Water connects bodily life, agriculture, settlement, industry and ecological continuity. Habitat concerns the spaces in which people live, work, recover and belong. Infrastructure organizes durable networks and common capacities. Mobility governs the movement of people, goods, information and opportunity across space. Materials follow matter through extraction, transformation, use, maintenance and return.

The six facets should therefore be read as a mesh. Energy shapes mobility; mobility reshapes habitat; habitat determines water demand; water and materials constrain production; infrastructure stabilizes or amplifies all of them. The purpose of the map is not to assign every phenomenon to one box, but to make the dependencies visible.

The labels remain working labels of Taxonomy A. They are operational handles for analysis, not a declaration that every historical naming question has been finally settled.

## 3. Reading the foundation through the conceptual layer

The conceptual layer A gives Material Base a complete diagnostic sequence.

**Definition** asks what physical base means in this architecture: resources, infrastructures and spatial organization that sustain civilization. **Function** asks what these arrangements enable: existence, coordination, production, care, movement and continuity. **Current State** examines how the base is presently organized, without reducing the answer to a single global condition. **Historical Evolution** traces how infrastructures, energy regimes, settlements and material practices became what they are.

**Core Pathologies** identifies structural failures such as linear dependency, fragility, exclusionary access or deferred maintenance. These are categories for inquiry, not universal diagnoses to be asserted without evidence. **Feedback Loops** asks how a material arrangement reinforces itself: how infrastructure can lock in a resource pattern, how spatial separation can increase mobility demand, or how underinvestment can create further fragility. **Dependencies** makes visible what each arrangement requires from other foundations and from the wider biosphere.

**Cross-Foundation Couplings** prevents Material Base from becoming an isolated engineering model. **Leverage Points** asks where a change in design, ownership, governance or practice could alter the wider pattern. **Regenerative Alternatives** describes healthier architectures without presenting a preferred future as already proven. **Transition Pathways** sequences change through experiments, institutions and learning. **Metrics / Signals of Health** distinguishes measurable conditions from internal ELYSIUM criteria and from aspirations.

The editorial layer E may organize examples around current paradigms, crises, transitions, catalysts, projects, risks and resources. It makes the chapter readable. It does not replace the conceptual layer A, and it does not convert a selection of examples into a complete ontology.

## 4. The six facets in relation

### Energy

Energy is the capacity through which matter is transformed and activity is sustained. The relevant question is not only how much energy is available, but how energy systems are built: who controls them, what infrastructures they require, which temporal patterns they impose and which dependencies they create.

An ELYSIUM reading must distinguish physical description, historical analysis, technological possibility and transition proposal. It must not treat “renewable” as an automatic synonym for regenerative. A technology can reduce one pressure while creating another dependency. The conceptual task is to examine the whole arrangement and its feedbacks.

### Water

Water is both a material flow and a condition of life. It connects household use, agriculture, industry, settlement, health and ecosystems. It also reveals the limits of purely sectoral thinking: an infrastructure that solves access in one location may alter conditions elsewhere.

P03 does not make universal quantitative claims about scarcity or abundance. It establishes the diagnostic frame in which water systems can be studied through source-backed conditions, governance choices, ecological relations and unequal access.

### Habitat

Habitat is the spatial form of daily life. It includes shelter, settlement, built environments and the relationship between private, collective and ecological space. Habitat influences health, time, belonging, exposure, access and the possibility of collective action.

A habitat is not healthy merely because it is efficient. Efficiency must be read alongside resilience, dignity, adaptability and the capacity to support relationships. These are architectural hypotheses of ELYSIUM and require distinction from empirical measures used to evaluate particular places.

### Infrastructure

Infrastructure is the durable capacity through which flows are organized. It includes physical networks, facilities, maintenance systems and, where relevant, the digital layers that coordinate material activity. Infrastructure is an architecture of possibility: it determines what can circulate, at what speed, with what reliability and for whom.

Infrastructure should not be treated as neutral background. Its standards, ownership, interfaces and maintenance regimes distribute power and vulnerability. Yet P03 must avoid turning this statement into a universal accusation. Each claim about a specific network or institution requires a source, a period and a defined scope.

### Mobility

Mobility concerns movement through space: people, goods, services, opportunities and the infrastructures that connect them. It is not identical to transport volume. A system can produce more movement while reducing meaningful access, increasing dependency or separating essential functions from daily life.

The Mobility pilot is a derived working example, not a source object and not a complete representation of Material Base. Its reservations remain active, especially around macroeconomic estimates and causal interpretations. P03 may use it to demonstrate method, but must preserve its derived status and provenance.

### Materials

Materials follow the physical substance of civilization across extraction, processing, construction, use, maintenance, repair and return. The language of circularity is useful only when the actual flows, losses, ownership patterns and recovery limits are made visible. Circularity is a transition direction, not a condition to assume.

Materials also connect the visible built world to less visible infrastructures of labour, logistics and knowledge. A material diagnosis therefore asks not only what is consumed, but how a society decides what is valuable, durable, disposable or worth repairing.

## 5. Couplings across the seven foundations

Material Base becomes civilizationally intelligible through its relations with the other foundations.

With **Vitality**, it raises questions about water, soils, health, habitats and biospheric conditions. The relation is not a license to make ecological causal claims without evidence. It is a requirement to examine whether a physical arrangement sustains or degrades the conditions of life.

With **Agency**, it connects infrastructure to production, work, value, finance and innovation. Material systems enable economic activity, but economic incentives also shape their design and maintenance. Neither side should be reduced to the other.

With **Cohesion**, it exposes how access, housing, mobility and territorial organization affect belonging and inequality. These effects must be studied in defined contexts rather than assumed from abstract categories.

With **Governance**, it examines regulation, common infrastructure, standards, ownership and collective arbitration. Material transitions are never only technical transitions; they are also decisions about authority, responsibility and time horizons.

With **Vision**, it connects the physical base to science, technology, planning, data and foresight. Knowledge can reveal hidden dependencies, but no model removes the need for judgment and democratic choice.

With **Consciousness**, it asks what a civilization believes matter is for. The physical base expresses implicit hierarchies of value: what is protected, extracted, shared, abandoned or repaired. This is an interpretive layer of ELYSIUM, not an empirical measurement disguised as one.

## 6. A.Material Base and B.Infrastructure

The ELYSIUM Taxonomy Federation records a primary correspondence between `A.F01 Material Base` and `B.F04 Infrastructure` through `ETF-0005`. The correspondence is useful because both structures address physical capacities, networks, energy, mobility and spatial organization. It is not a synonym and not a fusion.

Taxonomy A includes resources and the metabolism of civilization. Taxonomy B explicitly includes digital systems. The difference matters: a relation can support translation without erasing what each taxonomy sees that the other does not.

The deferred relation `ETF-S-001` connects `A.F01` to `B.F06 Environment`. It remains deferred. B.Environment is an autonomous node and cannot be reduced to Material Base. In Taxonomy A, environmental concerns may be distributed across Material Base and Vitality; in Taxonomy B, their organization follows a different structure. The correct operation is comparison with scope notes, not silent normalization.

This federative discipline protects the practical usefulness of both models. It also prevents the chapter from making a larger claim than its evidence allows.

## 7. From material pathology to transition

A regenerative transition begins by making the existing material metabolism legible. It asks where flows begin, where they accumulate, who benefits, who bears risk, which infrastructures lock in present patterns and where learning can occur without pretending that a single intervention solves the whole system.

The relevant alternatives may include circular practices, resilient habitats, distributed capacities, repaired infrastructures, lower-dependency mobility and forms of energy and material coordination aligned with the conditions of life. These are families of direction, not a single policy programme. Their value must be tested through context, evidence, feedback and reversibility.

The conceptual sequence keeps the transition disciplined. Definition prevents slogans. Historical Evolution prevents the present from appearing inevitable. Core Pathologies prevents isolated symptoms from being mistaken for causes. Dependencies prevent local optimization from creating distant harm. Leverage Points prevent action from dispersing across an undifferentiated field. Metrics prevent aspiration from presenting itself as proof.

The editorial layer adds projects, experiments, risks and resources. It can make transition concrete, provided each example retains its source status and scope. A project is evidence of an experiment, not evidence that the whole civilization has already changed.

## 8. Passage toward the faceted matrices

P03 establishes the foundation-level map. The next step is not to import six complete matrices automatically, but to select faceted work according to a defined question, scale, evidence state and intended use.

A reader should be able to ask of any Material Base passage:

1. Which facet is in focus?
2. At which scale is it being read?
3. Which layer—conceptual A or editorial E—is organizing the analysis?
4. Which statements are source-backed, derived, internal, provisional or unresolved?
5. Which relation to another foundation or taxonomy is being asserted?

If these questions remain answerable, Material Base can function as a foundation rather than a catalogue. It becomes a way to read the physical metabolism of civilization while preserving uncertainty, provenance and the sovereignty of both taxonomies.
